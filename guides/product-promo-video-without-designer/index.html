How to make a product promo video without a designer

Last updated: 1 November 2026

Short answer

You can make a basic 30-second product promo yourself in CapCut or Canva using phone footage, which takes roughly 6–15 hours for a first one [VERIFY]. If you would rather not, Akdeinz Motion’s Product Promo (30–45 s) is €1,290 excl. VAT, delivered in 7 working days in 16:9, 9:16 and 1:1.

Do it yourself

A simple promo needs one message, a few clean shots of the product and a clear call to action. Decide where it will run first (Instagram Reels, a product page, paid ads) because that sets the aspect ratio.

Tools

  • CapCut: Timeline editor with keyframes, captions and templates. A good fit for assembling phone footage into a vertical promo. Check commercial-use terms for templates and music [VERIFY].
  • Canva: Simple drag-and-drop video editor with brand kit and templates. Quick for text-led promos; less control over timing [VERIFY].
  • Jitter: Browser-based tool for animating product screenshots, UI or graphics into short clips [VERIFY].
  • After Effects: Maximum control for animated product graphics. Not realistic as a first tool for a one-off promo.

Steps

  1. Write the single sentence the video must get across, for example what the product does and for whom.
  2. Make a short shot list: the product alone, the product in use, one close-up detail, and the logo.
  3. Film on a phone in daylight, with a steady surface. Shoot vertical and keep extra space around the product so you can crop to 1:1 and 16:9.
  4. Assemble the clips in CapCut or Canva. Open with the most interesting shot; do not start with the logo.
  5. Add on-screen text for the main benefit and a call to action at the end. Many people watch without sound, so add captions.
  6. Add music you have the right to use in a commercial video, then export in 9:16, 1:1 and 16:9.

Realistic time for a first 30-second promo: about 1–2 hours filming and 4–10 hours editing and exporting, so roughly 6–15 hours in total [VERIFY]. Later promos go faster once you have a template.

A simple structure for a 30-second product promo

  • 0–3 seconds: a hook, usually the problem or the most striking shot.
  • 3–10 seconds: what the product is and the main benefit.
  • 10–22 seconds: the product in use or the key features, one idea per shot.
  • 22–30 seconds: proof or result if you have a real one, then the call to action and logo.

When DIY is fine

Doing it yourself is fine in these cases:

  • You are testing whether a product sells and the promo is disposable.
  • You already have good footage and only need to cut it together with text.
  • The video runs organically on your own channels and is replaced every few weeks.
  • You have more time than budget and want to learn basic editing.

Consider hiring when the promo will run as a paid ad for months, sits at the top of a product page, or when 6–15 hours of your week are worth more than the difference in price.
